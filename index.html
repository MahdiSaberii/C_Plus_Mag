ℂ+Mag: Harnessing Magnitude-Only and Complex Measurements for Improved Dynamic MRI Reconstruction with Learned Priors

Mahdi Saberi, Yaşar Utku Alçalar, Merve Gülle, Chetan Shenoy, Mehmet Akçakaya
University of Minnesota
Paper (IEEE) arXiv Code BibTeX

ℂ+Mag reconstructs each frame from its own complex k-space plus the magnitude-only k-space of neighboring frames. Those magnitudes come from data the scan already acquires, so it improves image quality at no extra scan time.

Background

Two magnitudes ≈ one complex measurement

A complex-valued measurement carries two real numbers: its real and imaginary parts. A magnitude-only measurement carries just one. For sparse signals, Akçakaya and Tarokh showed that two random magnitude-only measurements hold the same information as one random complex-valued linear measurement [1], which motivates reconstructing MRI from a mix of the two [2].

Think of telling someone where your friend is. A complex measurement is like saying “260 m north-east of the school”: a distance and a direction. A magnitude-only measurement keeps the distance but drops the direction, so one of them only says “260 m from the school”. Two of them, “260 m from the school and 210 m from the library”, narrow it down to two spots, and one of those is in the river. Just as common sense rules out the river, a prior such as sparsity rules out the wrong solution.

Abstract

Dynamic MRI: free and accurate auxiliary magnitude measurements

MRI reconstruction methods for undersampled k-space data naturally utilize complex-valued measurements. Parallel developments in sparse phase retrieval have shown that magnitude-only measurements may provide complementary information for signal recovery. However, their use in MRI reconstruction remains largely unexplored, due to lack of practical settings where informative magnitude measurements can be obtained without additional scan time. In this work, we investigate the use of auxiliary k-space magnitude information for accelerated steady-state dynamic MRI reconstruction, and demonstrate strong consistency of k-space magnitudes across time-frames. Building on this observation, we propose ℂ+Mag, a magnitude-informed physics-driven deep learning (PD-DL) reconstruction method. The proposed method employs an ADMM-based unrolling framework with a novel magnitude-aware data-fidelity formulation, where quadratically smoothed optimization and momentum-based updates are introduced to address the non-differentiability and non-convexity of the magnitude constraints. Experiments on retrospectively undersampled cine MRI and phase-contrast flow MRI datasets, as well as prospectively undersampled real-time cine MRI acquisitions, demonstrate improved artifact suppression, sharper anatomical recovery, and better preservation of phase information compared to conventional PD-DL methods, which is further supported through blinded expert reader evaluations.

Prospective undersampling

Real-time cine at R = 8, trained without fully sampled data

Free-breathing real-time cine has no fully sampled reference, so these models were trained with multi-mask SSDU self-supervision. Real-time imaging is a natural fit for ℂ+Mag: neighboring frames are acquired one after another, so their magnitudes stay reliable even with irregular heart rhythms.
